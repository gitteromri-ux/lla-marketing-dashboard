1 Where we started · 28–30 Sep
THE SETUP

3 ad sets, ~$590 / day

Set 01 interest + buyer audience, set 02 retargeting, set 03 a wide test with Julie's 65-second film. Facebook feed only.

THE GOOD NEWS

Intent was right

1 cart for every 21 visitors and 2 sales from 5 carts. The people we reached wanted the masterclass.

THE PROBLEM

CPM $352 · $17 per visit

The audience pool was small, so Meta charged a premium for every impression. At that price we could never reach 10–15 sales a day. The job: cut the cost per visit without losing the intent.

2 Five moves to bring the cost down · 1–4 Oct
1

Bigger pool: 11 → 41 interests

8 longevity & health, 14 education & self-development buyers, 19 supplements & training. A bigger qualified pool means a cheaper auction. Set 01 · Thu 1 Oct.

2

Every cold set needs 2 signals: interest AND buyer

Interest alone brings readers. We added a hard AND: Engaged Shoppers (people who clicked "Shop Now" on an ad in the past week) or top-25% household income. Pool 38–45M, all of them people who buy online.

3

Placements: Reels + Instagram feed in, junk out

Added full-screen Facebook Reels (sound on) and Instagram feed next to Facebook feed. Removed Marketplace, Stories, Audience Network, Messenger and the Reels overlay strip.

4

Every video in 3 formats

Julie's 65s film and the UGCs now run square (feed), vertical 9:16 (Reels) and landscape, so each placement gets a full-screen ad, not a boxed one. Same night: 2 sales from Reels.

5

3 sets → 6 sets, smaller budgets

Each set holds one intent level with its own budget ($70–$319). If one level underperforms we move money in hours without restarting the whole campaign.

3 What we learned on the way · and how we handled it
SET 03 · LOW INTENT

Broad audience = clicks without carts

Set 03 ran with no audience filter. Cheap reach, weak buyers. Fri 2 Oct: rebuilt to the 41 interests AND Engaged Shoppers. Pool went from ~140M random adults to ~80M online buyers.

OVERLAY · CHEAP CLICKS

Cheapest placement, zero carts

The Reels overlay strip delivered at $3–$10 CPM and flooded us with clicks, but none of them reached a cart. Sat 3 Oct: switched off in all 6 sets. CPM went back up on purpose: we now pay for people who actually watch.

THE 65+ DILEMMA

65+ buys, but was taking 35–40% of spend

65+ is the cheapest age group and produced sales, but it was swallowing the budget. Balance: 65+ stays in the wide sets 03, 04, 05; the core sets 01 and 06 are locked to 45–64. Both ages keep getting tested, neither eats the budget.

THE WEALTH FILTER

Top-10% income cuts the pool by 80%+

Top-10% households are the most likely VIP buyers, but the filter shrinks the pool from ~90M to ~16M and raises CPM. So it lives only in set 05. Sets 01, 04, 06 use top-25% income or Shoppers, so they stay big and cheap.

CREATIVE · LESS RISK

Spend goes to proven winners

Julie's 65s film is in every set. The UGCs that sold on small budgets (man v4, woman v3) get the weight. Banners only where they earned it: card-gold in 01, card-blue in 02, each with a new 15s Reels version with music; weaker banners paused. Sets 05/06 are video-only, so banner clicks no longer eat video traffic.

BLUEPRINT · SAME RULES

Applied to the Blueprint campaign

Fri 2 Oct: Blueprint prospecting sets got the same 41 interests AND buyer filter, ages 45–64. Sun 4 Oct: same placements (feed + Reels + Instagram feed) and Julie's 65s in 3 formats.
